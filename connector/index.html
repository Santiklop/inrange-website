Connector for Claude

OECD transfer pricing examples, inside Claude

Ask Claude a transfer pricing question and it can bring in the worked example from the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines that is closest to your facts, with its paragraph reference.

What it does

The connector lets Claude do three things with the examples published on this site: search them, read one in full, and list them. Claude decides when to use it. A typical exchange:

YouOur distributor is being converted from full-fledged to limited-risk. Does the group owe it anything for the conversion?

ClaudeAnswers the question, and adds the closest example from Chapter IX: the facts, what the Guidelines conclude, the paragraph numbers, and a link to the example on this site.

It is read-only and free. It needs no account, and nothing you ask is stored.

Add it to Claude

The connector's address is:

https://mcp.inrange.nl/mcp

In Claude on the web, desktop or phone

  1. Open Customize > Connectors.
  2. Select Add, then Add custom connector.
  3. Enter a name, for example OECD TP examples, and the address above. Select Continue.
  4. For authentication choose No sign-in, then select Add.
  5. In a chat, open the + menu, choose Connectors and switch it on.

On a Team or Enterprise plan, an Owner adds it once for the organisation under Organization settings > Connectors. Members then find it under Customize > Connectors. The Free plan allows one custom connector.

When the connector is listed in Claude's directory, you will also find it there as OECD Transfer Pricing Examples by inRange.

In Claude Code

claude mcp add --transport http oecd-tp-examples https://mcp.inrange.nl/mcp

What to ask

You do not have to mention the connector. When it is switched on, Claude uses it where a question calls for it. Some questions that work well:

  • Our distributor is being converted from full-fledged to limited-risk. Is there an OECD example on whether compensation is due?
  • What examples do the OECD TP Guidelines contain?
  • Show me the OECD examples on cash pooling.
  • Which example illustrates paragraph 9.70?

The three tools

ToolWhat it returns
search_examplesThe examples closest to a description of a transaction or issue: up to five in brief, then further candidates as single lines. A paragraph number also works.
get_exampleOne example in full: the parties, the events, the facts, the question, the conclusion the Guidelines support, and the reasoning with paragraph references.
list_examplesThe examples by chapter and topic, one line each, after a statement of what is covered.

All three only read. None of them can change anything, anywhere.

What is covered, and how it is labelled

About 150 cases: the same ones as the interactive walkthrough on this site. Every result carries one of three labels, so a draft or an illustration is never mistaken for the final OECD text.

  • OECD Guidelines 2022. A worked example from the 2022 edition: Chapters I, II, III, IV, VI, VIII, IX and X and their annexes.
  • OECD discussion draft, June 2026 (not final). The examples of the draft revision of Chapter VII on intra-group services.
  • inRange illustration. A few cases written by inRange to illustrate Chapter IX. They are not OECD examples.

Not included: brief illustrations of a sentence or two inside a paragraph, the three Chapter VII examples of the 2022 text, and Amount B.

The examples are paraphrased for learning. Each comes with its paragraph reference, so you can read the original.

Limits

  • Answers are short by design. A search returns summaries, and Claude fetches the full text of an example when it needs it.
  • When called directly, for example from Claude Code, one address may make 30 requests a minute.
  • The content follows the examples on this site and is updated when they are.

What it is not

inRange is an independent transfer pricing advisory firm. This connector is not affiliated with or endorsed by the OECD. The authoritative text is the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines themselves. Nothing the connector returns is tax advice.

Privacy

inRange stores nothing about your requests: no questions, no search terms, no addresses. The privacy policy has the details.
